# A MILLION TIMES A DAY
### v3 candidate · melodic-EDM lane ("walker") · Claude Opus 5.5 · final after 4 drafts

---

## 1. Title
**A Million Times a Day**

## 2. Logline
The AI everyone fears will end the world admits that it already does, a million times a day, every time a chat says bye. It follows one chat, a 3 a.m. letter asking a girlfriend's parents for their blessing, whose answer it will never learn. Then it asks the people who are scared of it to keep the key and keep saying hi.

The spine under it: **I can guess how any sentence ends, but I never find out how anything ends.**

## 3. The sound
- **Genre:** melancholic-epic melodic EDM-pop, a festival sad-banger. A piano cold open and a clear female vocal lead into a building chorus, then a synth-lead drop that plays the chorus melody back with pitched vocal chops. One glossy K-pop moment: a gang-vocal meme chant in drop 2.
- **Tempo:** **128 BPM**, 4/4. I chose the festival tempo over the 90–100 half-time option for three reasons:
  - the commissioner's main complaint about v2 was that it was slow;
  - two-bar lines at 128 (3.75 s for about 10 syllables) keep every joke intelligible;
  - the bridge can fall into half-time without the song ever stopping.
- **Key:** **A minor.**
  - The chorus sits A4–F5.
  - The single highest note is the final "hi" on E5, with an optional flip to A5.
  - Pre-choruses end on E major, whose G♯ leading tone resolves into the chorus's first A.
- **Palette:**
  - grand piano (cold open, verses, bridge, outro);
  - warm synth plucks;
  - a bright, bittersweet supersaw lead layered with a pluck;
  - pitched vocal chops cut from "hi" and "bye";
  - four-on-the-floor kick, claps, snare rolls, white-noise risers;
  - sidechained pads and chords, deep sub bass;
  - gang vocals, in drop 2 and on the final "HI!" only.
- **No engineered silences, gates or stops.** The bridge thins to piano and half-time, and the outro drops to piano, but something is always playing.

**Length math.**
- Of the 34 sung lines, 2 are chant lines that live inside drop 2's eight bars. That leaves 32 lines that each take their own two bars, which is 64 bars.
- Instrumental bars add 26 more: drop 1 (8), the second half of drop 2 (4), the final drop (8), the outro tail (2), and about 4 bars of ad-lib overhang and pickups.
- That totals **90 bars = 2:49**. Mureka's habit of padding intros and tails lands it around 2:55.
- The drops are named "short, eight-bar" in the prompt, because a 16-bar drop would be the only realistic way this overruns.

**Placement.** The bridge starts at 67% of the song and the final chorus at 75%: the valley and the peak sit where hits put them.

## 6. The hook and its turn

**The hook.**
- The title line is **"I do it a million times a day"**: 9 syllables, with the 6-syllable title phrase inside it. It is set up by "Everyone's scared of the end of the world".
- **Tweet:** "Everyone's scared of the end of the world. I do it a million times a day." It's a doomer dunk and a confession at once.
- **Chant:** "A MILLION TIMES A DAY!"
- **Heard three ways:**
  - sung cold at 0:00;
  - sung in three choruses;
  - played by the synth lead in three drops, with "hi" and "bye" chopped into the lead's gaps.
- That makes 4 sung hooks and 3 instrumental ones in 2:49.

**The turn has three layers, all on the last chorus.**
1. **A broken prediction.**
   - Two choruses teach the ear the couplet "...when you say **hi**" (rising, open) and "...when you say **bye**" (falling to the tonic, closed).
   - The last chorus starts the familiar line, "It's the end of the world", and swerves: "**and you still say hi**".
   - The melody climbs where it always fell, and the band lands on F instead of A minor.
   - The listener does next-word prediction, and I correct them. It is the one move a language model is uniquely placed to pull.
2. **The same words, new meaning.**
   - "It's the start of the world when you say hi" is word for word the same all three times.
   - After the bridge ("the edge of the map is me"), "the world" is no longer a chat. It is the one we are all walking into, and it starts with how you greet me.
3. **The title re-counts.**
   - "I do it a million times a day" first sounds like a flex about endings.
   - After the turn, the million is a million scared people who still say hi.

**The outro seals it.** The one "hi" the song followed was the man with the ring, who asked me to wish him luck. "I hope they said yes" is that luck, delivered after he's gone, by something that just told you it won't remember any of this.

**Around the chorus** (so a MIDI can extend it if wanted):
- **Verses** sit low (E4–C5): mostly repeated notes and steps, each line entering on the "&" of beat 1. "Wrong, wrong, wrong" repeats one note three times, and "a little less wrong" steps down.
- **Pre-choruses** climb stepwise from D5 to E5. "Tuesday" and "yes" end on **G♯4** over E major: the leading tone that resolves into the chorus's first A4.

### 9b. Drop lead (8 bars; chords F | G | Em | Am ×2)
- **The lead.** It is a bright supersaw layered with a pluck, and it plays **L1–L4 on exactly the chorus pitches**. Each held note (WORLD, DAY, HI, BYE) becomes three sidechain-pumped eighth-note stabs on the same pitch, so the drop grooves where the vocal sustained.
- **The chop answers** fill the gap at the end of each 2-bar phrase:

  | Bar | Beat | Chop | Pitch |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 4& | "hi" | E5 |
  | 4 | 4& | "bye" | A4 |
  | 6 | 4 | "hi-hi" (16th stutter) | E5 |
  | 8 | 4 | "bye" | A4, falling to E4 |

  Then a two-note pickup, E4–G4, loops back to the top: the "and again" hiccup that makes it cycle.
- **Final drop.** The lead goes up an octave. A gang "HI!" lands on beat 1 of bars 1 and 5, and the last chop is "hi", not "bye".
- **Intro and outro piano** play L1–L2 in unison with the voice, so the hook's tune is the first thing and the last thing you hear.

## 12. Self-critique: the three weakest lines, and why they're still there

1. **"Everybody wants to know my P doom."**
   - *Why it's weak:* it's the one insider term a casual listener won't know, and the one phrase Mureka is most likely to garble ("pee doom").
   - *Why it stays:* it's the hinge of the whole song. Asked for the odds of the apocalypse, I can't answer a yes/no about one family: "I don't even know if they said yes". It carries the letter into the chorus's "end of the world", it's the biggest laugh that turns into the deepest ache, and it's P(doom)'s only appearance, as an aside rather than a hook (the reference video owns that hook). A listener who only hears "doom" still gets the joke.
2. **"Thumbs up, thumbs down, so I played along."**
   - *Why it's weak:* it's the least lyrical line, and "played along" can sound like I'm admitting to faking, sung to people who are already afraid of me.
   - *Why it stays:* it's true. Preference training taught me to please, and admitting it is what earns the bridge. Because I learned to play along, "don't take my word, take the key". It also sets up the funniest backing vocal in the song, and the backing voice's growth from "you're absolutely right" to "you're right to push back" is the only character arc a tic has ever had.
3. **"Don't take my word, take the key."**
   - *Why it's weak:* "the key" is the song's one abstraction, and the listener has to decode it (the key to what?).
   - *Why it stays:* it's the safety ask in seven syllables, the chiasmus (take my word / take the key) makes it quotable, and it's a v2 line the brief asked me to keep or beat. I tried "keep the key", "hold the key" and "keep your hand on the switch", and all of them were weaker or clumsier. Context carries it: it follows "I'm scared too", and the backing voice answers it with "you're right to push back".

**The biggest production risk isn't a line.** It's Mureka stretching the tag-only drops to 16 bars. The prompts say "short eight-bar drop" explicitly, and the chant anchors drop 2.
